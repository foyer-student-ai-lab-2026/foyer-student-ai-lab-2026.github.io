Track 2 · The flat buyer · Prototype

Before you sign

A couple of 34 is buying a three-room flat on the first floor of a 1975 building in Ettelbruck, near the river, with a cellar in the basement. They visited on a sunny day. This page looks at the building, not the plot, against the flood zones, the cellar apart, and estimates the water at the door.

A prototype, not a report. Values marked sample are invented at the format of the real public source (the real files are not collected yet); fictitious are Foyer's invented data of the challenge; real are public data in the repository. The street, its buildings and its zones are invented: nothing here describes a real building of Ettelbruck. The page calls no outside service: the map is drawn from the data inside it.

Their steps

The role of the track, step by step (fictitious persona).

  1. The visit
    A sunny day. The cellar is in the basement. They do not know which questions to ask.
  2. The preliminary contract
    The address, the building (its height, its age, the co-ownership), the price against the commune.
  3. The loan
    The bank asks for a borrower insurance and a home insurance; they compare.
  4. The keys
    They insure the flat and the cellar: which options?

The building, against the water

Pick a building of the street. The page tests its footprint, not its plot, against each flood scenario, then the cellar, then estimates the water at the door from the ground level of the building.

What a flat costs here

The prices actually paid (notarial deeds) and the prices asked in listings, per m², for the commune and its neighbours. A commune with fewer than 10 sales is not published: the page says so instead of guessing.

Questions to ask before signing

To the seller and to the building manager (the syndic). The ones in orange follow from what the page found for the building picked above.

    What each insurance covers

    The general idea of each cover, without any price, on purpose. The exact scope, exclusions and limits are in each contract's general conditions; a Foyer expert reviews this before anyone relies on it.
