Pricing on fictitious data

A premium that follows the risk, factor by factor.

A fictitious home premium for an address and a building: a base, one factor per risk, each option priced apart, every factor explained in plain words with its source. A small frequency and cost model gives the place factor. A fairness check proves no sensitive attribute is read. All amounts are fictitious.

04 The model behind the place factor fictitious

Claims are simulated on the fictitious grid (5 years, about 10,000 cells), then a Poisson GLM is fitted per peril: log(frequency) = b0 + b1 · log(index / 100). The fitted b1 near 1 says the index carries the frequency; the mean cost is fitted apart. Code: model/fit_frequency_cost.py.

05 Fairness check

The premium is computed by one function. The check runs it here, in your browser: it records every attribute the function reads, and prices the same home under many invented profiles that differ only by sensitive attributes.

Limits, said plainly

  • A place factor can still act as a proxy: if a neighbourhood's fictitious index followed its income, the premium would too. The check below compares nothing of the kind: it would need public income data by commune (STATEC salaries by commune) to test.
  • The claims are simulated from the indices, so the model can only find what was put in. It shows the method, not a real effect.
  • The grid is invented: amounts and multipliers mean nothing about any real price.
  • A "not found" layer leaves its option unpriced rather than guessed.
