Track B · Home insurance

Insure the right risk, at the right price

The user is the future client. They give their address, see the risks of the place and the Mozaïk options that match them, with why, and a premium they can understand. Later, when a storm comes, they get the right advice. An advisor sees the same thing, but the client comes first.

The main role, step by step

  1. A family has just bought a house at the foot of a slope in Mersch and subscribes online on a Sunday evening.
  2. They type the address; the risks of the place appear, each with its source; what the base cover already handles.
  3. They compose the contract: each option useful, maybe, or not needed here, with why. The journey only asks what the data cannot tell (a cellar, valuables, solar panels not visible). The choice stays theirs.
  4. The premium is explained: what makes it go up or down, on a grid that is invented and says so. Never a real tariff.
  5. A year later, a MeteoLux storm warning covers their region: what to do now (garden furniture, the non-return valve, who to call), and what their contract covers.
Variants, if you prefer
  1. The agent preparing the quote sees the same page.
  2. The pricer: a frequency and cost model on the fictitious grid, and a fairness check (no sensitive attribute, nothing personal).
  3. The alert across the whole portfolio: how many homes are inside a warning area.
Your starting point: the table we sent you

Start from the table of risks, public data and Mozaïk covers in our email of 30 October, with the general conditions sent before it as the reference. Turn each line into a question of the journey, for this address: what does the data show here, which option to suggest, which exclusion to say. Then challenge it and go beyond it.

Questions to go further
  1. Theft: are there public statistics on burglaries by commune or region? Nothing localised was found in the first analysis: worth a search.
  2. Solar: does the solar potential map (Klima-Agence, check its licence) say, roof by roof, when Eco Energies makes sense? Are panels visible on the summer 2025 aerial image (ACT, CC0)?
  3. Buildings: do the footprints and heights (ACT) tell how isolated a house is, or what is exposed?
  4. Floods: does the scenario (HQ10, HQ100, extreme, AGE, CC0) change the advice, or the premium?
  5. Storms: which MeteoLux warnings (CC0, live, in FR, DE and EN) and which records since 1947 make a useful alert?

Fictitious Foyer data, now

The share of homes with each Mozaïk option per canton; the 500 m grid (relative indices for water damage, storm and hail, burglary, 100 being the national mean; the natural catastrophes option); homes per commune and canton (cellars, construction year, equipment); options chosen at subscription and journeys abandoned by life moment.

You can ask for

Fictitious frequency and average cost by peril, commune and year; claims by option; an exposure table. At the grain of the open data.

A first step, in one week
  1. Have an AI read the general conditions and fill a table per option (what it covers, excludes, the paragraph); check it by hand.
  2. Take ten addresses, compute the lines of the table for each, and draft a subscription page that suggests the options with one sentence of reason, and an invented premium explained factor by factor.
A good result shows: the future client understands why each option is suggested and what moves the premium; no question the data already answers; "fictitious" on every price; the choice left to the person; a storm alert that says what to do.
