Track A · Individuals

An address, before you decide

Someone arrives in Luxembourg, chooses where to live, and later buys a flat. Help them understand the risks of each address, what to insure, and what to ask, in their own language, before they decide.

The main role, step by step

  1. A 29-year-old engineer is hired in Luxembourg City and arrives in six weeks. She reads neither French nor German yet.
  2. She compares a studio in Esch-sur-Alzette and a two-room flat in Bertrange: rent, commute, neighbourhood, the risks of each address.
  3. She rents first: the landlord asks for tenant home insurance. What is compulsory, what does liability cover?
  4. Two years later she buys a flat in a 1975 building near a river: the building against the flood zones, the cellar apart, the price of the commune, the loan's insurance and the home insurance.
  5. One winter, an orange weather warning: what to do, who to call.
Variants, if you prefer
  1. Comparing two addresses before a move.
  2. The flat buyer, from the visit to the keys.
  3. An elderly person living alone: prevention, who to warn, the nearest help.
  4. The owner of several homes: the risks of each, side by side.
  5. The daily commute: the road segments, wildlife, ice.
Concrete questions
  1. For each address: flood zone and scenario, runoff after heavy rain, past floods, noise, an industrial neighbour, the nearest fire station.
  2. The building, not the plot: is it in the zone, is the cellar, how deep could the water be at the door?
  3. Rent and prices per commune; who lives there (communities, languages, ages).
  4. What must I insure, what is useful, what can wait? What should I ask the seller or the landlord?

Fictitious Foyer data, now

On the site: new home contracts by life moment ("arrived in Luxembourg", "buying a home": tenants, channel, language, days from moving in, journeys abandoned, options), the 500 m grid (water damage index, cellars), equipment per canton, road segments and commutes.

You can ask for

Claims by peril, commune and year; vehicles. Fictitious, aggregated, at the grain of the open data.

A first step, in one week
  1. Take two real addresses from BD-Adresses, one of them near a river, and compare them on one page: risks (the building, not the plot), rent and prices, community, commute, each line with its source.
  2. Write it in English and in one more language; add "what to insure when you arrive" and "what to ask before you sign".
A good result shows: a person who understands in five minutes what they are choosing, in their language; the building against the risk, the cellar apart; a reason and an action for each risk; and what the data does not know. Never a black-box score.

Going further. The full report of an address, as some services already do for France; the water depth at the door from the 3D buildings and the terrain; a live alert when a warning or a rising river touches the area. Write to: the organising team, who will bring in the team for individual customers and the home insurance experts.
