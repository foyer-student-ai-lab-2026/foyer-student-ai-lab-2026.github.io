My Risk Compass · Data dictionary

Fictitious Foyer data

What an insurer knows internally, invented and aggregated at the same grain as the public data: a smoothed 500 m grid (a heatmap you can put under any address), the communes, the cantons, and the main roads cut into 1 km segments. Homes and their equipment, the Mozaïk options people chose, relative claim indicators, vehicles, commutes between cantons, and the life moments behind new home contracts (who subscribes, through which channel, in which language).

Browse the columnsBack to the data

Read this first

  1. Everything is fictitious. The places are real (the commune boundaries are the official ones), the numbers are not. They are shaped loosely by real geography, so they look plausible: do not draw any conclusion about a real place from them.
  2. Aggregated only. No address, no household, no vehicle, no insurance policy, no claim record, no personal data. A claim indicator is an index (100 = national mean) over many homes or many kilometres; on the grid it is a smoothed mean over about 2 km² around the cell, shown only where that window holds at least 30 insured homes.
  3. No public data here. Weather, flood zones, terrain, roads, fire stations, accidents: take them directly from the real sources (data.public.lu, geoportail.lu, MeteoLux…), which are real and up to date. To cross the two, use the coordinates (grid cells, road segments), the commune or the canton.

The public data, real, to take from the source

Every address in the country is geocoded and public (ACT), and so is the cadastral parcel it sits on (PCN): cross the zones with the whole plot, not only the address point. Around it: flood and flash-flood maps, past flood events and water levels (AGE); terrain and LiDAR (ACT); land cover (LIS-L); weather warnings and records (MeteoLux); weather stations (ASTA); fire and rescue centres (CGDIS); SEVESO sites; traffic counts and roadworks; road accidents by canton (STATEC); OpenStreetMap; Copernicus. For the life moments: population by nationality and age by commune (RNPP), migrations and the 2021 census (STATEC), sale prices and rents by commune (Observatoire de l'Habitat). Most are on data.public.lu; check each dataset's licence on its page.
