Guidelines, for every track

Start from a real problem: the process, and the data you need, follow from it. Work in parallel: part of the group hunts for every open dataset that could help, part of it digs into the real case. Find the data first, then stay concrete: one person, one address, one company, one decision.

Who is involved

The people you help

A newcomer choosing where to live, a family buying a flat, someone subscribing home insurance online, the manager of a company with several sites.

Foyer, the organising team

The AI and Data Science team: your questions, the fictitious Foyer data, and the link to the right expert.

Foyer experts

Prevention for companies (large, medium and small: identify risks, help reduce them); home insurance, subscription and pricing; the team for individual customers.

The public data producers

The land registry (ACT), the water management agency (AGE), STATEC, MeteoLux, the rescue services (CGDIS), the business register's open data, OpenStreetMap contributors. Their data, their licences.

The problems underneath
01

The information exists, in pieces

Flood maps, buildings, weather, prices: each source holds one piece, on its own site, in its own format.

02

A plot is not a building

A parcel can touch a flood zone while the building stands outside it, and the other way round. Precision changes the answer.

03

People choose without knowing

Where to live, which options to take, which site to protect first: the decision is made before the risk is understood.

04

Prevention comes late

Advice usually arrives after the damage. Data can bring it before, with a clear reason and a simple action.

What not to do

Respect every licence

Read the licence on each dataset's page and cite the source. CC0 is free; CC BY needs the attribution; OpenStreetMap is ODbL: write "© OpenStreetMap contributors".

Respect robots.txt and terms

Do not scrape a site whose robots.txt or terms forbid it (the trade register's website is one), nor search engine results. Prefer the bulk download or the official API. In doubt, ask us first.

No personal data

Public data and the fictitious Foyer data only. Do not collect people's names, company directors or contact persons, and never try to re-identify anyone.

No client address to an outside service

When a report is made for a client, the address never leaves: no online geocoder or map service called with it. Download the whole layer, geocode on BD-Adresses, intersect locally. Query a commune or the whole country, never around a person.

How to ask a question
  1. Write to the organising team by email, one question per mail, with your track in the subject.
  2. Say what you tried, with the link to the data or the code, and what you need: a dataset, an expert, a check.
  3. Asking for fictitious Foyer data? Give the grain (500 m grid, commune, canton, road segment), the period and the columns you would like.
  4. We find the expert who fits the question and come back to you. No question is too small.
Sharing your work

We would love to learn from what you build and carry the best ideas further. A shape that makes it easy:

  1. A code repository under an open licence (MIT or Apache 2.0 for code, CC BY 4.0 for texts and data), that runs with one command from a fresh clone.
  2. A README.md in English: the problem and the person, the track, how to run it, one line per data source (name, link, licence, download date, what you take from it), what is fictitious, the limits, what is left to do.
  3. The scripts that download the open data (not the large raw files), the aggregated results they produce, and a dictionary of their columns. Ours show the shape, one script per source, free to reuse (MIT): the collection code (addresses and a local geocoder, building footprints and heights, flood zones, STATEC by commune, filed accounts, LEI, OpenStreetMap streets).
  4. No personal data, no client address, no API key in the repository.
