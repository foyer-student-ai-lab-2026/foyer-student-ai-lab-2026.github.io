The challenge: with public data, tell anyone what threatens their home, car and daily life, why, and what to do.

The questions people ask, and yours

  1. What are my main risks?
  2. Why am I exposed?
  3. What should I keep a close eye on?
  4. What can I concretely do to prevent these risks?
  5. How can I better protect my home, my car and my belongings?

Floods, storms, hail, fire, theft, ground movement, the road and its accidents: the scope is wide on purpose. How you frame it, for whom, is part of the challenge.

Imagine it. Build it.

Four illustrations run in the presentation: scan an address on a map of zones, the dangers reported on a daily route, the right insurance cover for the risks found, two addresses compared before a move. They are ideas, not the answer. What you build is yours to imagine, and AI can help you build it.

What you get

Real public data

Addresses, flood and flash-flood maps, terrain, land cover, weather warnings, roads, traffic, fire stations and more, taken directly from the source. See the catalogue.

Fictitious Foyer data

Aggregated as finely as the public data: a smoothed 500 m heatmap, communes, cantons, road segments. Homes, equipment, insurance options, claim indicators. No policy, no claim record, nothing individual. Data dictionary.

Mozaïk general conditions

Foyer's modular home insurance: a base, plus options to compose. To match covers to risks. PDF.

Rules of the game

No personal data

Public data and the fictitious data only. No scraping of personal data, no re-identification.

Explainable and actionable

A score alone is not enough: say why, and what to do. Each risk traceable to its data.

Open tools

Open-source or free-tier tools; respect each dataset's licence.

Fair

No discrimination on sensitive attributes. Advice is guidance, not certified expertise.
