The challenge: with public data and fictitious Foyer data, show anyone what threatens their home, car and daily life, explain why, and say what to do.

Start from a life moment, step into someone's shoes

Marketing starts with a person. Pick a moment of life, become the person living it: what they worry about, the words they use, how they want to subscribe. The message, the subscription journey, the site and its navigation follow, a little different for each.

01

Buying a flat

Sophie, 34, signs for a flat in Esch. She worries about the building, the cellar, the cost. She subscribes at signing, with her bank or an agent. Cross: flood and runoff maps, 3D buildings, sale prices.

02

Just arrived in Luxembourg

Ana, 31, moves from Lisbon for a job. Which commune, which language, what is compulsory? She needs it in her language, step by step. Cross: who lives in each commune, ages, rents, the risks of each place. Statistics tell the story of a place, never of a person.

03

The ones you imagine

A first home of your own, a first car, a baby, a retirement: the moments, and the people living them, are yours to imagine.

Step into their shoes: the moment shapes the message, the subscription journey, how the site and its navigation are designed. The fictitious data says, for each moment, who subscribes, through which channel and in which language.

The questions people ask, and yours

  1. What are my main risks?
  2. Why am I exposed?
  3. What should I keep a close eye on?
  4. What can I concretely do to prevent these risks?
  5. How can I better protect my home, my car and my belongings?

Floods, storms, hail, fire, theft, ground movement, the road and its accidents: the scope is wide on purpose. How you frame it, for whom, is part of the challenge.

Imagine it. Build it.

Four illustrations run in the presentation: scan an address and its cadastral plot on a map of zones, the dangers reported on a daily route, the right insurance cover for the risks found, two addresses compared before a move. They are ideas, not the answer. What you build is yours to imagine, and AI can help you build it.

What you get

Real public data

Addresses, flood and flash-flood maps, terrain, land cover, weather warnings, roads, traffic, fire stations and more, taken directly from the source. See the catalogue.

Fictitious Foyer data

Aggregated as finely as the public data: a smoothed 500 m heatmap, communes, cantons, road segments. Homes, equipment, insurance options, claim indicators, and the life moments behind new contracts. No policy, no claim record, nothing individual. Data dictionary.

Mozaïk general conditions

Foyer's modular home insurance: a base, plus options to compose. To match covers to risks.

Foyer experts

The AI and Data Science team, claims and operations experts.

Other Foyer data in mind? Tell us: we will see what we can share. More public data is yours to find.

Rules of the game

No personal data

Public data and the fictitious data only. No scraping of personal data, no re-identification.

Explainable and actionable

A score alone is not enough: say why, and what to do. Each risk traceable to its data.

Open tools

Open-source or free-tier tools; respect each dataset's licence.

Fair

No discrimination on sensitive attributes. Advice is guidance, not certified expertise.

What we will look at: many risks, crossed sources, clear reasons (never a black box), simple and concrete advice, responsible AI.
